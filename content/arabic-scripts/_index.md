Arabic calligraphy is not a single style but a rich tapestry of various scripts, each with its own history, rules, and aesthetic appeal. From the rigid geometry of Kufic to the flowing elegance of Thuluth, these scripts define the art form and shape how calligraphers express themselves.

| **[Kufic](/arabic-scripts/kufic/)** | One of the oldest Arabic scripts, characterized by angular, geometric forms and monumental presence. |

## How to Choose a Script

When selecting a script for your calligraphic work, consider these factors:

- **Purpose:** Monumental inscriptions and formal displays favor Thuluth and Kufic, while manuscripts benefit from Naskh's legibility.
- **Complexity:** Beginners often start with Naskh due to its straightforward rules, while Thuluth and Diwani demand advanced skill.
- **Aesthetics:** Choose based on the mood you want to convey—Kufic is geometric and bold, Thuluth is elegant and flowing, Diwani is ornamental and decorative.
- **Historical Context:** Understanding a script's origin and cultural significance enriches your practice and honors the tradition.
- **Medium & Tools:** Different scripts require different pen angles and tools; ensure your materials are suited to your chosen script.
