Thuluth (Arabic: ثلث, "one-third") is a script variety of Arabic calligraphy. It is one of the most important and elegant of all the classical scripts. Known for its clear structure and readability, it is often used for mosque decorations and Quranic headings.

### History and Characteristics
The Thuluth script was first formulated in the 11th century during the Abbasid dynasty and was later refined by Ottoman calligraphers. The name "one-third" refers to the ratio of the width of the pen to the length of the vertical strokes.
